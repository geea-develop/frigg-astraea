# TMO Research — Television Match Official (Rugby)

## What It Is

An additional match official positioned off-field with access to video replays, acting as a review layer for the on-field referee.

## Key Principles

- The referee remains the decision-maker — the TMO is a tool to assist, not override.
- Only invoked in specific, pre-defined circumstances — not everything is reviewed.
- Any match official can recommend a review (TMO, assistant referees, or the referee).
- Communication follows a structured protocol: "What, Where, Who."
- Designed to be time-bounded — efficient, not disruptive.

## The Flow

1. An event occurs (try scored, possible foul play, unclear kick).
2. Referee signals time-out ("Check, check").
3. Referee asks a specific, scoped question (e.g., "Is it a try — yes or no?").
4. TMO reviews multiple angles via video replay.
5. TMO provides a recommendation.
6. Referee makes the final decision and signals it.

## When It Can Be Used (Limited Scope)

- Grounding of the ball for a try
- Whether a kick at goal was successful
- Infringements in the build-up to a try (within 2 phases)
- Acts of foul play
- Clarifying sanctions for foul play

## Design Characteristics

- Accuracy without paralysis — improve decisions without stopping the game.
- Layered authority — TMO advises, referee decides.
- Bounded intervention — only specific triggers, not constant monitoring.
- Structured request/response — the reviewer answers what is asked, not more.
- The system doesn't eliminate bad calls entirely — it reduces them within acceptable cost (time).

## Observations

- The TMO does not act autonomously; it responds to requests or flags specific triggers.
- There is a clear separation between who observes, who requests, and who decides.
- The scope of review is deliberately limited to avoid slowing down the primary activity.
- The protocol is rigid by design — it prevents scope creep during a review.
- Trust in the system comes from transparency of the process (communication is broadcast to the audience).
- The TMO was introduced incrementally — starting narrow (tries only) and expanding over time.
- Even with TMO, the system accepts imperfection — it optimises for "better decisions within time constraints," not perfection.
